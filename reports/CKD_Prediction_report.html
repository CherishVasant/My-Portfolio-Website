Problem & Motivation

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) represents a massive global healthcare burden, marked by the gradual loss of kidney function. Left undetected, it progresses to end-stage renal disease, requiring dialysis or transplantation. Early diagnostic screening can halt or slow disease progression, but patients are frequently asymptomatic in early stages.

This project builds an automated, data-driven diagnostic tool to classify patient clinical records into CKD or non-CKD. The core objective is to design a highly accurate model that minimizes clinical false negatives (failing to identify a patient with CKD) while maintaining a minimal feature count. By restricting inputs to simple, cheap, and easily accessible clinical tests, we aim to deliver a model viable for rapid screening in resource-limited clinics.

Pipeline Overview

The project implements a structured five-step pipeline from raw patient features to hyperparameter-tuned prediction.

01. Clinical Data Cleaning
Missing values were categorized by frequency. Low missing values (<5%) were imputed using categorical mode or numerical mean. Columns with high missing values (>30%), such as red blood cells, received an explicit 'missing' category to avoid imputation bias. Skewed features were log-transformed.
02. Outlier Handling
Checked clinical normal bounds for physiological markers. Impossible outliers (e.g. Sodium = 4.5 mEq/L, which is fatal) were removed, while extreme, disease-related outliers (severe clinical indicators) were preserved to maintain vital medical training signals.
03. Healthcare-Specific Feature Engineering
Calculated Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) and constructed clinical indices, including a Comorbidity Score, Anemia Severity Score, Kidney Function Score, and Symptom Severity Score, capturing medical indicators of disease progression.
04. Feature Subset Versioning
Models were evaluated across three feature representations to study feature complexity vs. model performance: v0 (31 features, full dataset), v1 (11 clinical features), and v2 (5 core markers: Hemoglobin, Specific Gravity, Serum Creatinine, Hypertension, and Blood Glucose Random).
05. Training, Tuning, and Overfitting Profiling
Evaluated 26 classification models using Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation. Tuned hyperparameters (via Grid and Randomized search) to optimize recall. Profiling flagged overfitting based on the gap between training performance, CV performance, and prediction confidence.

Data & Preprocessing

The analysis is conducted on the Chronic Kidney Disease Dataset, containing clinical records for 400 patients with 30 raw and engineered variables. Key features include specific gravity (sg), serum creatinine (sc), blood urea (bu), hemoglobin (hemo), blood pressure (bp), and hypertension (htn).

To avoid information leakage, numerical features were scaled using a StandardScaler fitted solely on training splits, and categorical values were mapped using a LabelEncoder. The target column classification contains ckd and notckd classes, mapped to 0 and 1.

Model Training & Setup

Models were trained across multiple categories, including linear algorithms (Logistic Regression, Ridge, SGD), decision trees/ensembles (Decision Tree, Random Forest, Extra Trees, AdaBoost, Bagging, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM), SVMs (RBF, Linear, Polynomial kernels), K-Nearest Neighbors, Naive Bayes, Linear Discriminant Analysis, and Multi-layer Perceptron.

To profile overfitting, we calculate an Overfitting Risk category (LOW, MEDIUM, CRITICAL) for each classifier. A model is penalized and marked as CRITICAL if there is a perfect training performance score (F1 = 1.0) alongside a performance drop on validation splits, high prediction confidence (extreme probability output for incorrect predictions), or a large performance gap (F1 gap > 5%).

Hyperparameter optimization was executed for all key classifiers under a custom healthcare utility metric, which heavily penalties false negatives (optimizing recall over precision) to ensure high clinical sensitivity.

✦ Overfitting Risk Penalty

In the evaluation script, final scores were derived by taking a weighted clinical score (40% Recall, 25% F1, 20% Accuracy, 15% ROC AUC) and subtracting an overfitting penalty: 0.00 for LOW risk, 0.05 for MEDIUM risk, 0.15 for HIGH risk, and 0.30 for CRITICAL risk. This forces the selection of stable models over models with high test scores but severe overfitting indicators.

Key Learnings & Challenges

A primary engineering challenge was managing high missingness in critical indicators like Red Blood Cells (RBC), where imputing values would create dangerous artifact signals. Creating explicit "missing" categories preserved vital information while preventing artificial bias in decision boundaries.

Detecting data leakage was another crucial step: models trained on the full feature set (v0) quickly achieved perfect accuracy (100%), which was identified as a risk of proxy variable leakage. By running ablation trials across feature subsets (v1 and v2), we proved that the model could retain 98.75% classification performance with only 5 key variables, significantly reducing clinical cost and data requirement while maintaining screening integrity.

Summary

The project recommends an MLP or Gradient Boosting classifier trained on a subset of 5 core clinical features (v2+hyp) as the optimal diagnostic model.

By utilizing only easily testable variables (Specific Gravity, Serum Creatinine, Hemoglobin, Blood Glucose, and Hypertension history), the pipeline achieves a headline diagnostic accuracy of 98.75% and a clinical recall of 98.00%. This proves that an automated, high-sensitivity diagnostic screening tool can be successfully deployed in clinics, offering immediate clinical assessments without requiring complete, expensive patient lab profiles.
