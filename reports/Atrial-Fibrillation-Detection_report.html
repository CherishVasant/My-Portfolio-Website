Technical Report

ATRIAL FIBRILLATION DETECTION

Multi-Stage Hierarchical ECG Arrhythmia Classification with Deep dilated TCN-ResNets & Spectrogram-based EfficientNets

PyTorch Dilated TCN-ResNet EfficientNet-B2 NeuroKit2 SMOTE XGBoost

Problem & Motivation

Atrial Fibrillation (AF) is the most prevalent sustained cardiac arrhythmia, increasing the risk of ischemic stroke, heart failure, and systemic thromboembolism. Identifying AF from single-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) sensors (such as smartwatches and portable screening patches) is crucial for early detection.

However, automated detection faces three severe challenges: high signal variance due to electrode placement, motion artifacts (noise), and massive class imbalance (where healthy rhythms heavily outnumber cardiac anomalies). Standard end-to-end models often default to predicting the majority "Normal" class, missing critical arrhythmic episodes, or struggle to separate noise from genuine physiological signals.

Approach & Architecture

This project addresses these challenges via a Multi-Stage Hierarchical Inference Pipeline that separates the detection of healthy rhythms from the classification of specific anomalies.

  1. Signal Segmentation & Feature Fusion: The raw ECG recordings are split into 15-second windows (4,500 samples at 300 Hz). Hand-crafted physiological metrics are extracted using the NeuroKit2 toolkit (generating 39 heart-rate variability features).
  2. Stage 1 — Normal vs. Non-Normal Classifier: A hybrid deep neural network combining a Dilated 1D TCN-ResNet (for temporal sequences) and a Feature MLP (for HRV features) outputs the probability of a rhythm being abnormal. We tune the classification threshold to 0.3 to maximize sensitivity and prevent missed arrhythmias.
  3. 2D Spectrogram Extraction: For abnormal signals, we compute a 3-channel visual spectrogram ($224 imes 224 imes 3$) stacking: Channel 0 (Short-Time Fourier Transform, STFT), Channel 1 (Continuous Wavelet Transform, CWT), and Channel 2 (Recurrence Plot, RP).
  4. Stage 2 — Spectrogram CNN Subtype Classifier: The abnormal window spectrograms are processed by an EfficientNet-B2 convolutional architecture to determine the specific class: Atrial Fibrillation (AF), Other arrhythmia (O), or Noisy (~).

Data & Preprocessing

The pipeline is trained on the PhysioNet Challenge 2017 dataset, consisting of 8,528 single-lead ECG records. After windowing, we obtain 22,376 training windows and 5,548 validation/test windows.

Due to the severe class imbalance (Normal rhythms represent ~60% of recordings, while Noisy signals are <4%), SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) is applied during traditional machine learning baselines to balance the training distribution across the minority classes.

Key Learnings & Challenges

Fusing hand-crafted features with deep representations in Stage 1 dramatically improved accuracy for healthy rhythms but exposed a trade-off: false alarms. Since Stage 2 was only run on spectrograms where Stage 1 predicted "Non-Normal", any Stage 1 false alarm had no corresponding spectrogram representation. To solve this, the pipeline defaults Stage 1 false alarms to the most common non-normal subclass.

In future iterations, training a unified multi-task network directly on both 1D signals and 2D spectrograms could mitigate the misalignment between stages and eliminate the need for hardcoded fallback rules.

Final Approach & Results

Our final multi-stage hierarchical pipeline achieved a headline test Macro F1-score of 0.7720 and an Overall Accuracy of 85.98%. This outperforms the standard MLP baseline (61.68% F1) and XGBoost baseline (67.14% F1). Fusing deep 1D Dilated TCN-ResNets with hand-crafted HRV features and utilizing 3-channel visual spectrograms on EfficientNets proves highly effective for clinical-grade ECG arrhythmia monitoring.

The proposed hierarchical system was evaluated against targeted clinical thresholds:

Noisy (~) 0.6400 ≥ 0.80 ✗ FAIL
Evaluation Honesty
The model failed to reach target thresholds for the "AF" and "Noisy" categories. Since "Noisy" recordings represent a very small sample (80 windows total in the test set), the F1 score was highly sensitive to individual misclassifications (specifically 22 noisy samples missed and predicted as Normal due to Stage 1 thresholding).
