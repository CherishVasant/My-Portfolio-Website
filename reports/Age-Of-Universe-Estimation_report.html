Technical Report

Age of the Universe Estimation

Measuring the Hubble constant and the matter density parameter from Type Ia supernova distance moduli, and deriving the age of the universe under a flat ΛCDM cosmological model.

Astropy SciPy NumPy Cosmology Nonlinear Curve Fitting Pantheon+SH0ES

Research Question

This project analyzes observational data from the Pantheon+SH0ES Type Ia supernova sample to measure the Hubble constant (H₀), estimate the matter density parameter (Ωm), and derive an estimate of the age of the universe, by fitting a flat ΛCDM cosmological model to redshift and distance-modulus data.

The work was completed as "Project 2: Supernova Cosmology," a submission for the Summer School 2025 Astronomy & Astrophysics program at the Indian Space Academy, and is documented in hubble_parameter.ipynb and Report_SupernovaCosmology_677170.pdf.

Methodology

As implemented in hubble_parameter.ipynb:

  • Flat ΛCDM dimensionless Hubble parameter: E(z) = √(Ωm(1+z)³ + (1 − Ωm))
  • Luminosity distance (Mpc), by numerically integrating 1/E(z) with scipy.integrate.quad: dL(z) = (1+z) · (c / H₀) · ∫₀z dz′ / E(z′)
  • Theoretical distance modulus: μ(z) = 5·log₁₀(dL [Mpc]) + 25
  • Model fitting: non-linear least squares fit of μ(z) to the observed (z, μ, μerr) data via scipy.optimize.curve_fit, initial guess H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3. Fit twice: once with both H₀ and Ωm free, once with Ωm fixed at 0.3.
  • Age of the universe: t₀ = ∫₀∞ dz / [(1+z)·H(z)], computed numerically via quad, with H₀ converted to SI units via astropy.units and the result converted to gigayears.
  • χ² statistic used to build a parameter-space contour (H₀ vs. Ωm) with 1σ/2σ confidence regions: χ² = Σ [(μobs − μmodel) / μerr]²
  • Residual analysis: raw residuals plotted vs. redshift; normalized residuals plotted as a histogram with a Gaussian KDE overlay (scipy.stats.gaussian_kde).
  • Sample split test: dataset split at z = 0.1 into low-z and high-z subsamples, each independently fit for H₀ with Ωm fixed at 0.3, to check for redshift-dependent variation in H₀.

Additional relations were computed in an appendix but explicitly noted in the notebook as not directly related to the core project: lookback time vs. redshift, angular diameter distance (dA = dL/(1+z)²), H(z) = H₀·E(z) evolution, and scale factor a(t) = 1/(1+z) vs. lookback time.

Model assumptions
Spatial flatness (Ωk = 0), dark energy modeled as a cosmological constant with fixed equation-of-state w = −1, and large-scale homogeneity/isotropy — as stated in the accompanying report (Q6).

Data Sources

Dataset: Pantheon+SH0ES Type Ia supernova sample, loaded from the local file Pantheon+SH0ES.dat (space-delimited, 1,701 rows × 47 columns). Downloaded from the Pantheon+ GitHub data release (PantheonPlusSH0ES/DataRelease, 4_DISTANCES_AND_COVAR/Pantheon+SH0ES.dat).

Columns used for fitting: zHD (Hubble diagram redshift), MU_SH0ES (distance modulus, SH0ES calibration), MU_SH0ES_ERR_DIAG (uncertainty in distance modulus). The notebook verified 0 missing values and 0 duplicate rows.

Reference paper cited for comparison: Hu, J. P., Wang, Y. Y., Hu, J., & Wang, F. Y. (2023). "Testing the cosmological principle with the Pantheon+ sample and the region-fitting method." Astronomy & Astrophysics.

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Flat spatial geometry is assumed (Ωk = 0); relaxing this would introduce an additional curvature parameter and could change the fitted H₀ and Ωm.
  • Dark energy is modeled as a cosmological constant with constant equation-of-state w = −1; a dynamical dark-energy model would require more parameters and could shift the fitted age and H₀.
  • The universe is assumed homogeneous and isotropic at large scales; the report notes this could explain observed variation in H₀ across regions or redshifts, citing a directionally higher H₀ of 74.26 ± 0.39 km/s/Mpc reported by Hu et al. (2023).
  • Ωm was fixed at 0.3 in parts of the analysis to reduce parameter degeneracy, which directly affects the resulting H₀ estimate.
  • The low-z vs. high-z H₀ difference (73.01 vs. 73.85 km/s/Mpc) "may suggest" redshift-dependent effects, supernova population evolution, calibration differences, or local structure — stated as a possible implication in the notebook, not a conclusion.
  • Most supernovae in the sample lie at z < 0.1 (local universe), since these are brighter and easier to detect.

Quantifiable Scope

  • Dataset: 1 file (Pantheon+SH0ES.dat), 1,701 supernova records × 47 columns; 3 columns used directly in the fits.
  • 1 notebook (hubble_parameter.ipynb) producing 12 plots: redshift histogram, 2 Hubble diagrams (log and linear), model-fit overlay, χ² contour plot, residual scatter plot, residual distribution histogram, low-z/high-z comparison plot, plus 4 appendix plots.
  • 4 cosmological parameter fits performed: full-sample free fit, fixed-Ωm fit, low-z subset fit, high-z subset fit.
  • 2 age-of-universe values computed: 12.36 Gyr (full-sample fit) and 12.82 Gyr (fixed-Ωm fit).
  • 5 external reference values cited for comparison (SH0ES H₀, Planck 2018 H₀, Planck 2018 Ωm, Planck 2018 age, and Hu et al. 2023's full parameter set).
